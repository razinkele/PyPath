# Curonian Lagoon Scenario Definitions (2025-2035)

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** October 2026  
**Study:** Ecosystem-Based Fisheries Management for Curonian Lagoon  
**Modeling Period:** 2025-2035 (11 years)

---

## Executive Summary

Five policy-relevant management scenarios have been defined for the Curonian Lagoon ecosystem to support decision-making in fisheries management. These scenarios span the plausible management policy space and reflect key tradeoffs between conservation, productivity, economic value, and spatial protection.

**Scenarios at a glance:**
1. **Baseline (Business-as-Usual)**: Status quo 1.0× fishing effort
2. **Fishing Reduction (50%)**: Conservation-focused stock rebuilding
3. **Fishing Increase (30%)**: Productivity-focused yield maximization
4. **Spatial Closure (25%)**: Marine protected area with spillover effects
5. **Selective Harvest**: Species-selective fishing for economic optimization

---

## SCENARIO 1: Baseline Business-as-Usual (2025-2035)

### Scenario Identification
- **Scenario ID:** BAU_001
- **Name:** Baseline_BAU_2025_2035
- **Short Code:** BAU
- **Management Goal:** Maintain status quo fishing pressure (reference scenario)

### Management Strategy
Maintain current fishing effort at 2015-2018 average levels (1.0× historical multiplier) across all commercial species.

### Fishing Parameters

| Species | Fishing Multiplier | Rationale |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| Bream (*Abramis brama*) | 1.0× | Current exploitation |
| Perch (*Perca fluviatilis*) | 1.0× | Current exploitation |
| Roach (*Rutilus rutilus*) | 1.0× | Current exploitation |
| Vimba (*Vimba vimba*) | 1.0× | Current exploitation |
| Zander (*Sander lucioperca*) | 1.0× | Current exploitation |

### Spatial & Temporal Characteristics
- **Spatial closure:** None (full lagoon access)
- **Seasonal restriction:** None (year-round fishing)
- **Area fished:** 100% of lagoon

### Environmental Assumptions
- **River discharge (Nemunas):** Historical mean conditions (2000-2020 average)
- **Water temperature:** Seasonal variation per historical norms
- **Nutrient loading:** Assumed stable at 2020 levels
- **Climate scenario:** No climate forcing; business-as-usual conditions

### Ecological Rationale
The baseline scenario represents the null hypothesis — what happens if current management practices continue without modification. This provides the reference point against which alternative scenarios are evaluated.

### Expected Outcomes

#### Biomass
- **Mean total biomass:** ~2,100-2,400 tonnes (relative to model calibration)
- **Trend:** Relatively stable, minor fluctuations
- **Risk:** Potential gradual decline if recruitment becomes limiting

#### Catch
- **Annual mean catch:** 12-15 tonnes/year
- **Catch composition:** Bream 40%, Perch 30%, Zander 15%, Roach 10%, Vimba 5%
- **Total 11-year catch:** ~140-165 tonnes

#### Ecosystem Health
- **Trophic level of catch:** 2.6-2.8 (indicator of balanced fishing pressure)
- **Coefficient of variation in catch:** 0.25 (moderate variability)
- **Ecosystem stability:** Baseline (reference)

### Policy Implications
1. **Status quo sustainability:** Baseline allows assessment of whether current practices are sustainable
2. **Economic continuity:** Maintains baseline economic returns to fishing communities
3. **Risk assessment:** Identifies trends that may require intervention
4. **Reference point:** Essential comparison for evaluating alternative strategies

### Key Assumptions
- Fishing practices remain constant (gear, effort distribution, temporal patterns)
- No technological change in catching efficiency
- Environmental conditions remain within historical range
- No invasive species introductions
- Management compliance remains at current level

### Uncertainties
- **Recruitment variability:** Inter-annual variation in year-class strength not precisely quantified
- **Environmental drivers:** Nemunas discharge may shift due to climate change
- **Adaptation:** Fishers may modify practices (Allee effects, spatial redistribution)
- **Data quality:** Catch statistics 2001-2018 aggregated by species, not gear

## SCENARIO 2: Fishing Reduction 50% (Conservation Focus)

### Scenario Identification
- **Scenario ID:** CONS_002
- **Name:** Fishing_Reduction_50pct
- **Short Code:** RED50
- **Management Goal:** Stock rebuilding through effort reduction (ecosystem-based approach)

### Management Strategy
Reduce total fishing mortality by 50% to allow depleted stocks to recover and restore ecosystem resilience. This scenario reflects a conservation-first policy prioritizing long-term sustainability over short-term yields.

### Fishing Parameters

| Species | Fishing Multiplier | Current Effort | Reduction |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|-----------|
| Bream (*Abramis brama*) | 0.50× | 100% | 50% reduction |
| Perch (*Perca fluviatilis*) | 0.50× | 100% | 50% reduction |
| Roach (*Rutilus rutilus*) | 0.50× | 100% | 50% reduction |
| Vimba (*Vimba vimba*) | 0.50× | 100% | 50% reduction |
| Zander (*Sander lucioperca*) | 0.50× | 100% | 50% reduction |

### Spatial & Temporal Characteristics
- **Spatial closure:** None (uniform reduction across entire lagoon)
- **Seasonal restriction:** None (proportional year-round reduction)
- **Effort reduction mechanism:** Reduced number of fishing days, reduced net mesh sizes, or catch quotas

### Environmental Assumptions
- Same as Baseline (historical mean discharge, seasonal variation)

### Ecological Rationale
Eutrophication and historical fishing pressure have altered Curonian Lagoon trophic structure. A 50% reduction in fishing mortality allows:
1. **Recruitment enhancement:** Higher spawning stock biomass → increased recruitment
2. **Predator rebuilding:** Allows zander and perch populations to expand
3. **Ecosystem stability:** Reduces vulnerability to environmental variability
4. **Genetic diversity:** Larger populations maintain allelic variation

### Expected Outcomes

#### Biomass
- **Mean total biomass:** 2,900-3,200 tonnes (+35-40% vs. baseline)
- **Trajectory:** Initial recovery phase (years 1-5), stabilization (years 6-11)
- **Species-specific:** Bream +40%, Zander +50%, Perch +35%

#### Catch
- **Annual mean catch:** 6-8 tonnes/year (reduced from 12-15)
- **Total 11-year catch:** ~70-90 tonnes
- **Economic impact:** -45-50% gross revenue; -25-30% when accounting for price increases from scarcity

#### Ecosystem Health
- **Trophic level of catch:** 2.9-3.1 (higher, reflecting less selective fishing)
- **Coefficient of variation:** 0.20 (improved stability)
- **Size-at-capture:** Increased mean size of captured individuals
- **Recovery time to stability:** 6-8 years

### Policy Implications
1. **Long-term sustainability:** Stock rebuilding reduces collapse risk
2. **Ecosystem services:** Enhanced predation control, nutrient cycling
3. **Livelihood transition:** Requires economic support for fishing communities
4. **Regional markets:** Reduced supply may increase local fish prices
5. **Spillover potential:** Larger populations provide spillover recruitment to adjacent coastal waters

### Key Assumptions
- Fishing effort can be effectively reduced by 50% through enforcement
- Ecological compensation: 50% effort reduction → 30-40% biomass increase (not 50%)
- No illegal or unreported fishing
- Fishers do not shift effort to other species outside the 5 commercial species
- Social acceptance of income reduction during transition period

### Uncertainties
- **Recruitment curve:** Assumes stock-recruitment relationship remains unchanged
- **Economic hardship:** May drive increased illegal fishing
- **Adaptation:** Fishers may target other species (pike, eel, carp)
- **Market effects:** Price response to reduced supply not quantified

## SCENARIO 3: Fishing Increase 30% (Productivity Focus)

### Scenario Identification
- **Scenario ID:** PROD_003
- **Name:** Fishing_Increase_30pct
- **Short Code:** INC30
- **Management Goal:** Maximize short-term economic yield

### Management Strategy
Increase fishing effort by 30% above current baseline to maximize annual catches and economic returns. This scenario prioritizes immediate productivity over long-term stock sustainability.

### Fishing Parameters

| Species | Fishing Multiplier | Current Effort | Increase |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|----------|
| Bream (*Abramis brama*) | 1.30× | 100% | +30% |
| Perch (*Perca fluviatilis*) | 1.30× | 100% | +30% |
| Roach (*Rutilus rutilus*) | 1.30× | 100% | +30% |
| Vimba (*Vimba vimba*) | 1.30× | 100% | +30% |
| Zander (*Sander lucioperca*) | 1.30× | 100% | +30% |

### Spatial & Temporal Characteristics
- **Spatial closure:** None (intensive fishing throughout lagoon)
- **Seasonal restriction:** None (extended fishing season or daily effort)
- **Effort increase mechanism:** More fishing boats, longer nets, increased fishing days

### Environmental Assumptions
- Same as Baseline

### Ecological Rationale
Under favorable environmental conditions and efficient harvest, the Curonian Lagoon productivity may support higher extraction rates. This scenario tests the ecosystem's capacity for intensified exploitation while accounting for density-dependent effects and recruitment limitation.

### Expected Outcomes

#### Biomass
- **Mean total biomass:** 1,600-1,900 tonnes (-25-35% vs. baseline)
- **Trajectory:** Initial increase in catch (years 1-3), then decline as stocks become depleted
- **Species-specific:** Bream -40%, Zander -35%, Perch -25%

#### Catch
- **Annual mean catch:** 16-20 tonnes/year (+30-35% vs. baseline, years 1-3)
- **Catch trend:** Declining after year 5 as stocks deplete
- **Total 11-year catch:** ~150-170 tonnes (higher than baseline due to front-loading)
- **Economic return:** +30-40% years 1-3, declining to -10-15% by year 11

#### Ecosystem Health
- **Trophic level of catch:** 2.4-2.6 (lower, more selective fishing of small individuals)
- **Coefficient of variation:** 0.35-0.45 (increased instability, high volatility)
- **Size-at-capture:** Decreasing mean size indicates recruitment overfishing
- **Risk indicators:** By year 8-11, high probability of stock collapse scenarios

### Policy Implications
1. **Economic boom-bust cycle:** Short-term revenue gains followed by long-term losses
2. **Fishery instability:** Increased catch variability threatens livelihood security
3. **Recruitment overfishing:** Risk of depleting breeding stock below viable levels
4. **Spillover loss:** No spillover to adjacent areas due to local depletion
5. **Social disruption:** Unsustainable practices may force industry restructuring

### Key Assumptions
- Fishing efficiency improvements allow 30% effort increase without proportional cost increases
- Catch-per-unit-effort remains constant for first 3 years (density dependence delayed)
- No regulatory intervention despite warning signs
- Price stability despite supply increase
- No market saturation or demand reduction

### Uncertainties
- **Compensatory growth:** Effect of reduced population density on individual growth rates
- **Ecosystem collapse:** Nonlinear transitions to alternative stable states
- **Predator-prey dynamics:** Effects of predator depletion on lower trophic levels
- **Recruitment compensation:** Potential for strong year classes to offset harvest

## SCENARIO 4: Spatial Closure 25% (Marine Protected Area)

### Scenario Identification
- **Scenario ID:** MPA_004
- **Name:** Spatial_Closure_25pct
- **Short Code:** MPA25
- **Management Goal:** Ecosystem protection with spillover benefits to fishing areas

### Management Strategy
Establish a marine protected area (MPA) covering 25% of the lagoon area (northern sanctuary zone), with no fishing allowed. Fishing continues at baseline effort (1.0×) in the remaining 75% of lagoon area, resulting in a proportional reduction of accessible fishing grounds.

### Fishing Parameters

| Species | Fishing Multiplier (75% area) | Rationale |
|---------|-------------------------------|-----------|
| Bream (*Abramis brama*) | 0.75× | 25% area unavailable for fishing |
| Perch (*Perca fluviatilis*) | 0.75× | 25% area unavailable for fishing |
| Roach (*Rutilus rutilus*) | 0.75× | 25% area unavailable for fishing |
| Vimba (*Vimba vimba*) | 0.75× | 25% area unavailable for fishing |
| Zander (*Sander lucioperca*) | 0.75× | 25% area unavailable for fishing |

### Spatial & Temporal Characteristics
- **Protected area:** 25% of lagoon (northern sanctuary)
- **MPA location:** Northern Curonian Lagoon (shallow, high breeding habitat)
- **MPA area estimate:** ~850 km² × 0.25 = ~212 km²
- **Fishing area:** Remaining 75% of lagoon (~637 km²)
- **Spillover mechanism:** Adult migration and larval dispersal from sanctuary to fished areas

### Environmental Assumptions
- Same as Baseline
- Environmental connectivity: Larvae and juveniles dispersed from sanctuary by currents/tidal exchange

### Ecological Rationale
Marine protected areas provide multiple benefits for ecosystem management:
1. **Biomass spillover:** Population growth in sanctuary → adult emigration to fished areas
2. **Larval subsidy:** Larvae produced in sanctuary replenish recruitment in fished areas
3. **Refuge habitat:** Juveniles concentrate in sanctuary, reducing predation mortality
4. **Genetic diversity:** Larger sanctuary populations maintain genetic variation
5. **Precautionary principle:** Buffer against uncertainty in population dynamics

### Expected Outcomes

#### Biomass in Protected Area
- **Sanctuary biomass:** 800-1,000 tonnes (reference population)
- **Trajectory:** Growth phase (years 1-6), equilibrium (years 7-11)
- **Average size:** Larger individuals due to fishing cessation

#### Biomass in Fished Area
- **Fished area biomass:** 1,500-1,800 tonnes (baseline 2,100 - spillover effects moderate decline)
- **Spillover effect:** +300-400 tonnes over 11 years relative to fishing reduction alone
- **Timing:** Spillover increases years 3-11 as sanctuary population builds

#### Catch
- **Annual mean catch:** 10-13 tonnes/year (25% area reduction)
- **Total 11-year catch:** ~115-145 tonnes
- **Economic impact:** -15-20% compared to baseline; +40-50% compared to fishing reduction scenario

#### Ecosystem Health
- **Trophic level of catch:** 2.7-2.9 (similar to baseline)
- **Coefficient of variation:** 0.22-0.24 (improved stability vs. baseline)
- **Size structure:** Bimodal distribution (large in sanctuary, smaller in fished area)
- **Recruitment:** +15-25% higher juvenile recruitment from sanctuary by year 6-11

### Policy Implications
1. **Win-win potential:** Economic losses smaller than proportional fishing reduction; ecosystem benefits significant
2. **International precedent:** Aligns with CBD marine spatial planning targets (10% protected by 2020, 30% by 2030)
3. **Fisheries support:** Spillover effects maintain catch relative to simple closures
4. **Conflict potential:** Spatial restriction creates localized pressure on fished areas
5. **Scientific monitoring:** Requires ongoing measurement of spillover effects

### Key Assumptions
- 25% area closure achievable through enforcement and boundary demarcation
- Spillover rate: ~5-10% of sanctuary biomass emigrants reach fished areas annually
- No spillover loss outside lagoon (open boundary assumption relaxed)
- Fishers accept reduced area in exchange for spillover recruitment benefits
- MPA boundary remains stable over 11-year period

### Uncertainties
- **Larval dispersal:** Connectivity between sanctuary and fishing grounds variable
- **Fisher compliance:** Risk of illegal fishing in protected area
- **Alternative impacts:** Possible concentrating predation pressure in fished boundaries
- **Climate effects:** Environmental variability may suppress spillover effects in some years

## SCENARIO 5: Selective Species Harvest (Economic Optimization)

### Scenario Identification
- **Scenario ID:** SELEC_005
- **Name:** Selective_Species_Fishing
- **Short Code:** SEL
- **Management Goal:** Maximize economic value per tonne through species-selective harvesting

### Management Strategy
Shift fishing effort toward high-value species (Bream, Zander) while restricting harvest of low-value forage species (Roach, Perch). Implements selective gear (larger mesh sizes, hook selection) to target economically valuable size classes.

### Fishing Parameters

| Species | Fishing Multiplier | Value/kg | Rationale |
|---------|-------------------|----------|-----------|
| Bream (*Abramis brama*) | 1.20× | €8-12/kg | High-value target; marketable size classes |
| Perch (*Perca fluviatilis*) | 0.70× | €3-5/kg | Low-value, small; focus on juveniles |
| Roach (*Rutilus rutilis*) | 0.60× | €2-4/kg | Forage species; competitor with perch |
| Vimba (*Vimba vimba*) | 0.80× | €5-7/kg | Medium value; migration vulnerability |
| Zander (*Sander lucioperca*) | 1.30× | €12-15/kg | Highest value; apex predator rebuilding |

### Spatial & Temporal Characteristics
- **Spatial closure:** None (full lagoon access, but selective gear reduces catchability)
- **Seasonal restriction:** Zander spawning protection (March-April: multiplier 1.0×) to maintain breeding stock
- **Gear selectivity:** Larger mesh nets (≥40 mm), selective hook types for demersal species

### Environmental Assumptions
- Same as Baseline

### Ecological Rationale
Economic optimization recognizes that not all fish have equal value. Strategic harvest targeting high-value species while protecting forage fish yields:
1. **Economic efficiency:** Higher revenue per tonne captured
2. **Trophic support:** Reduced harvest of forage species maintains feeding opportunities for predators
3. **Predator rebuilding:** Higher Zander multiplier supports apex predator recovery
4. **Ecosystem function:** Reduced harvest of small perch and roach allows density-dependent predation regulation
5. **Livelihood viability:** Higher economic return per boat required for fishery viability

### Expected Outcomes

#### Biomass
- **Total biomass:** 2,200-2,500 tonnes (similar to baseline, but altered composition)
- **Bream biomass:** +20-25% (increased target)
- **Zander biomass:** +30-40% (increased target, high reproductive value)
- **Perch biomass:** +30-40% (fishing reduction allows recovery)
- **Roach biomass:** +40-50% (fishing reduction, reduced predation)

#### Catch
- **Annual mean catch:** 10-13 tonnes/year (total biomass similar to baseline)
- **Economic value:** €90-140k/year (+35-45% compared to baseline €65-95k)
- **Catch composition:** Bream 45%, Zander 25%, Roach 15%, Perch 10%, Vimba 5%
- **Total 11-year catch:** ~115-145 tonnes

#### Ecosystem Health
- **Mean trophic level:** 2.8-3.0 (higher due to zander increase)
- **Trophic efficiency:** Improved energy transfer through food web
- **Coefficient of variation:** 0.24-0.26 (good stability)
- **Predator-prey balance:** Zander predation on small fish regulates forage populations

### Policy Implications
1. **Livelihood sustainability:** Higher per-unit value maintains fishing industry viability with lower total catch
2. **Market differentiation:** Premium positioning for high-value Curonian lake fish
3. **Ecosystem services:** Apex predator rebuilding enhances ecosystem resilience
4. **Social equity:** Selective approach rewards more skilled/efficient fishers
5. **Compliance incentives:** Higher per-fish value increases monitoring and reporting compliance

### Key Assumptions
- Selective gears (mesh size, hooks) can effectively target desired size classes of each species
- Market demand exists for species-differentiated products (Bream, Zander premium vs. Roach bulk)
- Zander spawning grounds concentrated enough to protect March-April
- Fishers can adapt to new gear requirements without major capital costs
- Bycatch/unwanted-catch of protected species remains low with selective gears

### Uncertainties
- **Market dynamics:** Price premiums for selective harvest may not materialize if supply increases
- **Gear efficiency:** Selective gears may reduce catchability below predicted multipliers
- **Ecological compensation:** Reduced roach/perch harvest may increase bycatch through density-dependent effects
- **Predator-prey stability:** Zander population increase may exceed system carrying capacity in some scenarios
