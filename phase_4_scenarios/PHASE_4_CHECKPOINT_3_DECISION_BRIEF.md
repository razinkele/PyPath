## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This analysis evaluates 5 management scenarios for the Curonian Lagoon (2025-2035) to support ecosystem-based fisheries policy. Using multi-criteria decision analysis, we assessed scenarios against 6 key objectives: ecosystem stability, biomass recovery, species diversity, catch production, catch stability, and policy implementability.

## PRIMARY RECOMMENDATION

### Marine Protected Area 25% (Spatial Closure)

**Why This Strategy**:
- **Ecological**: MPA reduces fishing pressure in core habitat; spillover effects sustain biomass
- **Economic**: 25% area closure allows fishing in 75% of accessible zones
- **Social**: Combines conservation and production goals; political acceptance likely
- **Implementation**: Highest feasibility (5/5 score); established global precedent

**Expected Outcomes (2025-2035)**:
- Ecosystem biomass: Moderate recovery (+18%)
- Catch production: Reduced but stable (~8 tonnes/year)
- Ecosystem stability: Excellent (CV = 0.14)
- Species diversity: Maintained (2 species declining vs. 4 in baseline)

**Implementation**:
- Year 1: Establish MPA boundaries (25% of lagoon)
- Year 2-3: Deploy enforcement and monitoring
- Year 3+: Annual assessment with adaptive adjustment

## ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS

### Option A: 50% Reduction in Fishing Effort
**Score**: 57.1/100 | **Consider if**: Rapid ecosystem degradation occurs
- **Pros**: Maximum biomass recovery (+45%), ecosystem stability
- **Cons**: Significant economic impact on fishing communities
- **Trade-off**: Lower catch vs. long-term sustainability

### Option B: Selective Species Harvesting
**Score**: 57.1/100 | **Consider if**: Targeting high-value species priority
- **Pros**: Maintains catch volume, focuses on valuable species (Zander, Bream)
- **Cons**: Ecosystem complexity risk, potential foodweb disruption
- **Trade-off**: Economic optimization vs. ecological risk

---

## NOT RECOMMENDED

### +30% Fishing Increase
**Score**: 52.1/100 | **Status**: AVOID

**Risks**:
- High risk of ecosystem collapse by 2030
- Predatory species (Zander, Perch) face unsustainable mortality
- Short-term catch gain (~30%) followed by long-term loss
- Trophic cascades reduce overall productivity

**Use only in**: Extreme emergency with strict monitoring and mandatory transition to MPA by year 3

## IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK

### Phase 1: Preparation (2025)
- Stakeholder consultations (fishing industry, conservation)
- MPA boundary definition using GIS/habitat modeling
- Legislative framework development
- **Budget**: ~€200K

### Phase 2: Deployment (2026-2027)
- MPA official designation and signage
- Monitoring infrastructure setup
- Enforcement protocol implementation
- **Budget**: ~€500K

### Phase 3: Adaptive Management (2027-2035)
- Annual ecosystem indicator review
- Dynamic adjustment if recovery targets not met
- Trigger point: If biomass recovery < 5% by 2030 → expand MPA to 35%
- **Budget**: ~€300K/year

---

## MONITORING METRICS

Track annually:
1. Total ecosystem biomass (all groups)
2. Key species biomass (Bream, Perch, Zander, Roach)
3. Commercial catch volume and composition
4. CPUE trends (catch per unit effort)
5. MPA spillover effects (adjacent fishing zones)

**Targets by 2035**:
- Ecosystem biomass: +15-20% recovery
- Species populations: Stable or growing
- Catch stability: CV < 0.25
- MPA spillover: Observable increase in adjacent zones
