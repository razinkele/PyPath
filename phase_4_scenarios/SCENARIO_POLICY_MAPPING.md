## Overview

This document maps the five defined scenarios to specific policy questions and decision contexts. Each scenario is designed to provide evidence for particular stakeholder concerns and policy decisions.

## Policy Questions and Corresponding Scenarios

### Question 1: Can we maintain current catch indefinitely?

| Aspect | Answer | Evidence Source | Key Finding |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------------|
| **Policy Question** | Is 1.0× current effort sustainable over 11 years? | **Baseline (BAU) scenario** | Uncertain; requires monitoring |
| **Decision Context** | Status quo impact assessment | Baseline trajectory | Potential gradual decline if recruitment becomes limiting |
| **Stakeholder** | Fishing communities, industry | Baseline biomass trend | Moderate risk; ecosystem indicators needed |
| **Action Triggers** | If catch declines >10% in years 5-8 → intervention needed | Red flag monitoring | Switch to RED50 or MPA25 if declining |

**Rationale:** The Baseline scenario tests whether 1.0× effort can be sustained. If biomass remains stable and catch continues at 12-15 t/year, status quo is sustainable. If biomass declines and catch drops, intervention becomes urgent.

**Monitoring Metrics:**
- Annual catch volume by species
- Spawning stock biomass (SSB) trends
- Recruitment indices (juvenile density surveys)
- Trophic level of catch (declining = overfishing signal)

### Question 2: How much can stocks recover if we reduce fishing?

| Aspect | Answer | Evidence Source | Key Finding |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------------|
| **Policy Question** | What is the potential biomass recovery if effort drops to 0.5×? | **Reduction 50% scenario** | Biomass +35-40% by year 11; SSB recovery strong |
| **Decision Context** | Conservation target setting; stock rebuilding potential | RED50 trajectory | Mean biomass increases from ~2,200 t to ~3,050 t |
| **Stakeholder** | Environmental NGOs, conservation scientists | Recovery rate | 6-8 year recovery phase; stabilization by year 11 |
| **Action Triggers** | If recovery <25% by year 6 → stock health worse than expected | Model validation | Compare model predictions to biological surveys |

**Rationale:** Conservation stakeholders want to know if restrictions can actually rebuild stocks. The RED50 scenario quantifies recovery potential: 0.5× effort allows 35-40% biomass increase, plus improved ecosystem stability (CV down to 0.20).

**Monitoring Metrics:**
- Spawning stock biomass (SSB) trend (target: +40% by year 6)
- Mean size-at-capture (target: increasing)
- Reproductive output (egg production, fecundity)
- Ecosystem resilience indicators

---

### Question 3: What if we increase fishing to maximize short-term yields?

| Aspect | Answer | Evidence Source | Key Finding |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------------|
| **Policy Question** | Can we increase annual catch to 16-20 t/year sustainably? | **Increase 30% scenario** | Short-term YES (years 1-3); long-term NO (years 8-11) |
| **Decision Context** | Economic development pressure; yield maximization request | INC30 trajectory | Front-loaded catch followed by collapse risk |
| **Stakeholder** | Economic development agencies, fishing industry seeking growth | Revenue pattern | +30-40% revenue years 1-3; -10-15% by year 11 |
| **Action Triggers** | If catch declines >25% by year 7 → economic losses exceed initial gains | Boom-bust cycle warning | Recommend switching to MPA25 or SEL before year 5 |

**Rationale:** Productivity-focused stakeholders want evidence on yield maximization. INC30 shows unsustainable boom-bust: years 1-3 deliver high catch, but by year 8-11, stocks collapse and catches fall below baseline, resulting in net economic loss.

**Monitoring Metrics:**
- Annual catch volume (watch for declining trend after year 3)
- Catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) decline (indicator of overfishing)
- Recruitment failure signals (year-class strength indicators)
- Economic revenue (cumulative losses by year 11)

### Question 4: Can we protect 25% of lagoon and maintain reasonable catches?

| Aspect | Answer | Evidence Source | Key Finding |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------------|
| **Policy Question** | Will spillover effects from MPA compensate for 25% area loss? | **Spatial Closure 25% scenario** | Partially; catch down 15-20% but ecosystem stronger |
| **Decision Context** | Marine spatial planning; MPA effectiveness debate | MPA25 spillover dynamics | Spillover increases years 3-11; recovery trajectory visible |
| **Stakeholder** | Conservation + fishing balance advocates, regional governments | Win-win assessment | Lower catch than BAU, but much higher than RED50 |
| **Action Triggers** | If spillover <5% of sanctuary production by year 6 → MPA connectivity issue | Spillover validation | Survey larval/adult emigration rates |

**Rationale:** Spatial planning stakeholders want evidence on MPA feasibility. MPA25 shows spillover can partially offset fishing area loss: catch drops 15-20% (vs. proportional 25% if no spillover), and ecosystem indicators improve significantly.

**Monitoring Metrics:**
- Sanctuary biomass trend (should be highest of all scenarios)
- Spillover rate to fished areas (5-10% of sanctuary emigrants annually)
- Juvenile recruitment in fished areas (higher due to larval subsidy)
- Fisher acceptance and compliance

### Question 5: How do we maximize economic return while protecting ecosystem?

| Aspect | Answer | Evidence Source | Key Finding |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------------|
| **Policy Question** | Can selective targeting achieve high revenue with lower total catch? | **Selective Species Fishing scenario** | YES; €90-140k/year vs. €65-95k (BAU) |
| **Decision Context** | Economic viability of conservation; market-based approach | SEL economic efficiency | 35-45% higher revenue per tonne with 20-30% lower total catch |
| **Stakeholder** | Economic planners, sustainable fishing advocates, premium market developers | Value optimization | Ecological AND economic win compared to BAU |
| **Action Triggers** | If market prices don't support premium (Bream/Zander) → strategy collapse | Price monitoring | Develop market guarantees or certifications |

**Rationale:** Sustainable development stakeholders want evidence that conservation need not mean poverty. SEL shows species-selective approach can deliver comparable catch volume (10-13 t/year) at 35-45% higher economic value by targeting Bream and Zander over forage species.

**Monitoring Metrics:**
- Economic revenue per tonne (target: €8-12/kg for Bream/Zander)
- Market prices by species (target: premium maintained)
- Bycatch and selectivity (target: <5% unwanted catch)
- Ecosystem trophic structure (mean TL should increase)

## Scenario Recommendations by Stakeholder

### Conservation NGOs & Environmental Regulators
- **Primary Scenario:** Fishing Reduction (RED50)
- **Secondary Scenarios:** Spatial Closure (MPA25), Selective (SEL)
- **Decision Logic:** Focus on ecosystem recovery and stability; evaluate MPA25 as "softer" alternative with spillover benefits
- **Key Metrics:** Biomass recovery, ecosystem stability, recruitment success

### Fishing Industry & Local Fishers
- **Primary Scenario:** Baseline (BAU)
- **Secondary Scenarios:** Selective (SEL), Spatial Closure (MPA25)
- **Decision Logic:** Maintain livelihood; consider SEL for higher per-unit value without drastic effort reduction
- **Key Metrics:** Annual catch, economic revenue, livelihood stability

### Regional Economic Development Agencies
- **Primary Scenario:** Fishing Increase (INC30)
- **Secondary Scenarios:** Selective (SEL), Baseline (BAU)
- **Decision Logic:** Consider short-term benefits vs. long-term risks; SEL offers better risk profile
- **Key Metrics:** Annual revenue, economic stability, employment

### Marine Spatial Planning & Regional Governments
- **Primary Scenario:** Spatial Closure (MPA25)
- **Secondary Scenarios:** Selective (SEL), Baseline (BAU)
- **Decision Logic:** Balance conservation (CBD targets) with fishing viability; spillover benefits reduce economic impact
- **Key Metrics:** Area protection, spillover effectiveness, stakeholder acceptance

### Adaptive Management & Monitoring Teams
- **Primary Scenario:** Baseline (BAU)
- **Secondary Scenarios:** All scenarios (comparative monitoring)
- **Decision Logic:** Baseline provides reference; compare actual data to scenario predictions
- **Key Metrics:** Catch trends, biomass trajectories, ecosystem indicators

## Scenario Comparison for Decision Support

### If Goal = Maximum Economic Yield
**Ranking:** INC30 (years 1-3 only) > SEL > BAU > MPA25 > RED50
- **Caveat:** INC30 unsustainable long-term; front-load gains, then collapse
- **Recommended:** SEL if must balance economics + sustainability

### If Goal = Maximum Ecosystem Recovery
**Ranking:** RED50 > MPA25 > SEL > BAU > INC30
- **Recovery potential:** RED50 fastest, MPA25 good spillover benefits
- **Recommended:** MPA25 if need economic compromise

### If Goal = Maximize Economic Return per Tonne
**Ranking:** SEL (€90-140k) > INC30 (€95-140k short-term) > BAU (€65-95k) > MPA25 (€60-85k) > RED50 (€35-55k)
- **Best long-term:** SEL (sustainable + high value)
- **Recommended:** SEL with market development support

### If Goal = Ecosystem Stability (lowest risk)
**Ranking:** RED50 (CV=0.20) > MPA25 (CV=0.22-0.24) > SEL (CV=0.24-0.26) > BAU (CV=0.25) > INC30 (CV=0.35-0.45)
- **Most stable:** RED50 (conservation pays dividends)
- **Recommended:** MPA25 if must maintain higher catch

### If Goal = Social Acceptance (minimal disruption)
**Ranking:** BAU (no change) > SEL (gear change, positive value) > MPA25 (area restriction) > INC30 (short-term positive) > RED50 (income cut)
- **Least disruptive:** BAU (status quo); SEL (reframed as opportunity)
- **Recommended:** SEL with communication/training support
