# IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP: CURONIAN LAGOON MPA 25%
## 2025-2035 Phased Management Plan

**Date**: October 2026  
**Revision**: 1.0  
**Status**: Ready for Government Adoption

---

## EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW

This roadmap outlines the 10-year implementation path for Marine Protected Area 25% management of Curonian Lagoon. The plan spans three phases: Planning (2025), Deployment (2026-2027), and Adaptive Management (2028-2035).

**Total investment**: €3.1M over 10 years  
**Expected return**: €85-95M in sustained ecosystem services + fishing value  
**Key success factor**: Stakeholder engagement + adaptive monitoring

---

- Monthly consultation meetings
- Feedback integration report

**Timeline**: February-June 2025

**Partners**: Baltic Marine Institute, cartography specialists

## PHASE 2: DEPLOYMENT & ENFORCEMENT (2026-2027)

### Objective
Activate MPA, establish enforcement infrastructure, conduct first ecosystem assessment.

### Key Activities

#### 2.1 MPA Official Designation
**Timeline**: January 2026  
**Lead agency**: Ministry of Environment  
**Partners**: European Commission (CFP coordination)

**Deliverables**:
- Ministerial decree officially designating MPA
- International notification (HELCOM, IUCN World Database)
- Official signage at MPA perimeter (buoys, markers)
- Public announcement & media campaign
- Russia transboundary coordination (if feasible)

**Budget**: €20K  
**Success metric**: Official designation by January 2026

---

#### 2.2 Enforcement & Monitoring Infrastructure
**Timeline**: January-June 2026  
**Lead agency**: Fisheries Enforcement Directorate  
**Partners**: Coast Guard, Ministry of Defense, research institutions

**Infrastructure elements**:
- GPS vessel tracking system (all licensed fishing boats)
- 2-3 enforcement patrol boats (MPA-dedicated)
- Satellite monitoring (synthetic aperture radar for illegal fishing detection)
- Port monitoring (catch documentation before landing)
- Stationary monitoring stations (5-7 locations for fish population surveys)
- Observer program (trained monitors aboard select fishing vessels)

**Deployments**:
- Random patrols (3-5 times/week during fishing season)
- Seasonal intensity adjustment (spring/fall peak fishing periods)
- Joint patrols with Russian counterparts (if transboundary agreement)

**Personnel**:
- 15-20 enforcement officers (trained MPA-specific protocols)
- 5-7 scientific monitors (ecosystem data collection)
- 1-2 coordination managers

**Budget**: €400K  
**Success metrics**:
- Infrastructure operational by June 2026
- Personnel trained & deployed
- <2% illegal harvest rate documented

## PHASE 3: ADAPTIVE MANAGEMENT (2028-2035)

### Objective
Monitor ecosystem recovery, implement adaptive adjustments, sustain long-term productivity.

### Key Activities

#### 3.1 Annual Ecosystem Monitoring & Assessment
**Timeline**: Continuous, each January-March  
**Lead agency**: Scientific Advisory Board (VGTU lead)

**Annual cycle**:
- January: Data compilation and quality check
- February: Ecosystem model re-calibration + scenario analysis
- March: Stakeholder briefing and trigger evaluation
- April-December: Monitoring continues for next year

**Key decisions evaluated annually**:
- Is ecosystem biomass recovering ≥5%/year? (Target: +18% by 2035)
- Are key species populations stable or growing?
- Is spillover occurring in adjacent zones?
- Is enforcement compliance maintained (≥98%)?
- Are trigger thresholds approaching (decision points)?

**Deliverables**:
- Annual Status Report (30-50 pages scientific)
- Executive Summary (5-10 pages policy-ready)
- Stakeholder briefing presentation
- Media summary (1-2 pages, public communication)
- Trigger evaluation recommendation to minister

**Budget**: €80K/year  
**Success metric**: Reports published within 60 days of monitoring conclusion (by March 31 annually)

---

#### 3.2 Adaptive Management Decision Framework
**Timeline**: Applied annually based on monitoring triggers

**Decision triggers & responses**:

| Metric | Target | Trigger for Action | Response | Timeline |
|--------|--------|-------------------|----------|----------|
| Ecosystem biomass recovery | +15-20% by 2035 | <5% growth by end 2030 | Expand MPA to 30-35% | Decision by Q2 2031 |
| Key species populations | Stable or growing | >10% decline × 2 years | Targeted management + research | Immediate + 6-month study |
| Spillover CPUE | Observable by year 7 | No increase by year 7 | Reassess MPA boundaries | 6-month boundary review |
| Invasive species biomass | Stable or declining | >20% increase | Enhanced control + research | Immediate + funding request |
| Enforcement compliance | ≥98% | <95% two consecutive years | Enforcement intensity increase | Immediate budget reallocation |
| Industry stability | Catch ≥7 tonnes/year | Catch <6 tonnes/year | Extended support programs | Decision by Q2 annually |

**Decision process**:
1. Scientific Advisory Board evaluates metrics vs. triggers (February)
2. If trigger reached, SAB recommends action to Minister (early March)
3. Minister consults stakeholders (mid-March)
4. Government decision/adjustment announcement (by April 1)
5. Implementation begins (May 1)

**Budget**: Included in monitoring (€80K/year) + decision-specific costs

**Success metric**: All trigger evaluations completed on schedule; decisions made within 60 days

---

#### 3.3 MPA Boundary Adjustment (if needed)
**Timeline**: As triggered by monitoring (likely 2030-2032 based on recovery rate)

**Scenarios for adjustment**:

**Scenario A: Recovery exceeds expectations (unlikely but possible)**
- Action: MPA boundaries remain at 25% (success achieved)
- Rationale: No need to expand if targets being met
- Timeline: Reassess every 3 years

**Scenario B: Recovery on target** (most likely)
- Action: MPA remains 25%
- Rationale: Ecosystem recovery proceeding as modeled
- Timeline: Continue monitoring, no change

**Scenario C: Recovery slower than target** (possible ~30% probability)
- Trigger: <5% growth by end 2030
- Action: Expand MPA to 30-35% (add fishing-free zone)
- Rationale: Additional protection needed for recovery goals
- Timeline: Decision by Q2 2031; implementation by 2032
- Stakeholder consultation required (3-month process)
- New legislative amendment needed
- Expected outcome: Accelerated recovery by 2035

**Scenario D: Recovery stalled** (low probability ~10%)
- Trigger: <1% growth by 2032, or invasive species surge
- Action: Comprehensive review of causes
- Investigation options: Climate change? Pollution? Invasive species? Enforcement failure?
- Response options: Larger MPA (40-50%), targeted restoration, pollution controls, invasive removal
- Timeline: 12-month investigation; new policy by late 2032

**Budget**: €50K per boundary adjustment (planning + legal amendments)

---

#### 3.4 Spillover Effects Monitoring & Documentation
**Timeline**: Ongoing focus 2028-2035, particularly years 3-7

**Spillover research focus**:
- Fish abundance in zones adjacent to MPA (acoustic telemetry, visual surveys)
- CPUE changes in fishing zones adjacent to MPA (catch per unit effort)
- Species composition in adjacent zones (shift toward larger, older fish?)
- Economic spillover (do fishermen actually benefit?)

**Measurement methods**:
- Fixed acoustic receivers along MPA boundary (track fish movement)
- Quarterly research trawls in adjacent zones (population size/composition)
- Monthly fisheries data analysis (CPUE trends adjacent vs. distant zones)
- Annual fishery-dependent surveys (do fishermen observe spillover?)

**Key question**: Do spillover effects materialize as model predicted?

**Expected outcome**: 
- By year 5-7: Observable increase in fish abundance adjacent to MPA
- By year 10: CPUE in adjacent zones higher than baseline
- Validation: Model predictions confirmed or adjusted

**Budget**: €40K/year (included in monitoring)

**Success metric**: If spillover documented, confidence in model increases; if not, investigation triggers (Scenario D above)

---

#### 3.5 Long-Term Sustainability Assessment (Midpoint & Final)
**Timeline**: Formal assessments every 5 years (2030, 2035)

**2030 Midpoint Assessment** (March 2031):
- Are we on track to achieve 2035 targets?
- Is 25% MPA sufficient or is expansion needed?
- Industry stability: Are fishermen adapting successfully?
- Stakeholder satisfaction: Is the arrangement working?
- Climate/environmental changes: Are new pressures emerging?

**Deliverables**:
- Comprehensive 5-year review report
- Recommendation: Continue as-is, expand MPA, or adjust policies?
- Media/stakeholder communication

**2035 Final Assessment** (March 2036):
- Did MPA 25% achieve ecosystem recovery (+18% biomass)?
- Are key species populations stable?
- Is catch production sustainable long-term?
- Is industry economically viable?
- Is the system resilient to future pressures?
- Recommendations for 2035-2050 management

**Deliverables**:
- Final comprehensive assessment
- 2035-2050 long-term management strategy
- Public communication & celebration (if successful)
- Lessons learned document (for other ecosystems)

**Budget**: €100K for each (2030 + 2035)

## CONTINGENCY PLANS

### If Industry Rejects MPA (Low-Medium Probability: 20-30%)

**Early warning signs** (year 1):
- <80% compliance with fishing restrictions
- Continued catch in MPA zones despite enforcement
- Formal complaints to government/parliament

**Response escalation**:
1. Enhanced stakeholder dialogue (intensive industry consultation)
2. Enforcement intensity increase (more patrols, stronger penalties)
3. Support program expansion (higher financial assistance)
4. Media campaign (public opinion/peer pressure on non-compliance)
5. If escalation fails by 2028: Policy reassessment (possible MPA boundary reduction or alternative strategy)

---

### If Ecosystem Recovery Slower Than Projected (Medium Probability: 30-40%)

**Early warning signs** (2027-2029):
- Biomass recovery < 3% per year
- Key species populations declining vs. stabilizing
- Spillover effects not visible by year 5

**Response escalation**:
1. Research investigation (why is recovery slower?)
2. Monitoring intensification (monthly instead of quarterly)
3. Root cause analysis (pollution? invasives? climate?)
4. Policy options consideration:
   - Expand MPA to 30-35%
   - Add complementary management (invasive control, pollution reduction)
   - Adjust timelines (extend recovery window to 2040)
5. Stakeholder consultation on expanded strategy

---

### If Climate Change Accelerates (Low-Medium Probability: 25%)

**Early warning signs**:
- Annual temperature anomalies exceed Baltic-wide projections
- Species range shifts observable (warm-water species arriving early)
- Hypoxia events more frequent or severe
- MPA recovery trajectory disrupted

**Response options**:
1. Climate adaptation research (working group formed by 2028)
2. Habitat restoration (focus on thermal refugia, shallow areas)
3. MPA boundary adjustment (protection of climate-resilient areas)
4. International coordination (transboundary climate adaptation)
5. Integrated ecosystem management (combine MPA with pollution/restoration)

## CONCLUSION

This roadmap provides a structured, phased path to implement MPA 25% management for Curonian Lagoon through 2035. Success depends on:

1. **Scientific credibility**: Rigorous monitoring, transparent decision-making, peer review
2. **Stakeholder engagement**: Early, ongoing, honest communication with industry, conservation, government
3. **Institutional commitment**: Long-term budget protection, ministerial coordination
4. **Adaptive flexibility**: Willingness to adjust strategy based on monitoring evidence
5. **International cooperation**: HELCOM alignment, EU coordination, Russian engagement (where feasible)

**The path is clear. Implementation is achievable. The time to begin is now.**
