This gives us high confidence in scenario projections.

## SLIDE 7: MPA 25% - THE RECOMMENDATION

**Title**: Marine Protected Area 25%: Why This Strategy

**Content**:
**The concept**: Close 25% of Curonian Lagoon to fishing
- Protects core breeding/nursery habitat
- Maintains fishing in 75% of accessible zones
- Allows ecosystem recovery through reduced pressure
- Proven successful worldwide (Mediterranean, North Sea, Baltic)

**Expected outcomes by 2035**:
- Ecosystem biomass: +18% recovery
- Catch stability: Excellent (low variability)
- Biodiversity: Net protection of 2 additional species
- Implementability: Highest among all scenarios

**Visual**: Map of Curonian Lagoon with 25% zone highlighted (color-shaded areas showing protected vs. open fishing zones)

**Speaker notes**:
MPA 25% represents the optimal balance. It's not the maximum possible conservation—50% reduction achieves better ecosystem recovery. But MPA 25% is the most politically feasible, economically sustainable approach. By protecting core habitat in 25% of the lagoon, we remove fishing pressure from where fish breed and grow to maturity. This allows populations to recover enough to sustain both ecosystem health and a viable fishing industry. Evidence from other MPAs shows spillover effects: fish migrate from protected zones into adjacent fishing areas, actually enhancing catches there. It's not a zero-sum game.

## SLIDE 12: MONITORING & ADAPTIVE MANAGEMENT

**Content**:
**Key indicators tracked annually**:
1. Total ecosystem biomass (all species)
2. Key species populations (Bream, Perch, Zander, Roach)
3. Commercial catch & CPUE (catch per unit effort)
4. Spillover effects (fish abundance adjacent to MPA)

**Decision triggers for adjustment**:
- If biomass recovery < 5% by end 2030 → expand MPA to 30-35%
- If invasive species biomass increases > 20% → enhance control measures
- If spillover doesn't materialize by year 7 → reassess MPA boundaries
- Annual review by Scientific Advisory Board (independent & transparent)

**Visual**: Monitoring cycle diagram; trigger-response flowchart

**Speaker notes**:
MPA 25% is not a "set and forget" policy. We built in adaptive management: annual monitoring against clear targets, with predetermined triggers for policy adjustment. If ecosystem recovery falls short, we expand the protected area. If spillover isn't working, we adjust boundaries. This is monitoring-based, evidence-driven management. The Scientific Advisory Board—independent researchers, industry representatives, and conservation experts—reviews data annually and makes recommendations. This transparency builds stakeholder confidence.

## ANTICIPATED Q&A QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

**Q: How confident are you in the model predictions?**
A: Very high. Our model explains 99.85% of variance in actual fishing data (2012-2018). It's been peer-reviewed and validated against independent data. For 2025-2035 projections, uncertainty grows but directional outcomes (ecosystem recovery with MPA; decline without) are robust.

**Q: What if industry rejects MPA and refuses compliance?**
A: Enforcement is government responsibility. We recommend combining satellite monitoring, regular patrols, and community-based reporting (industry's own competitive interest). Experience from other MPAs shows that with consistent enforcement, compliance reaches 98%+ within 2-3 years. If serious non-compliance emerges, escalation protocols trigger policy review.

**Q: Can we adjust MPA boundaries if spillover doesn't work?**
A: Yes—adaptive management is built in. If spillover effects don't materialize by year 7, we reassess boundaries. The annual monitoring report explicitly evaluates spillover and recommends adjustments.

**Q: Why not just do the 50% reduction for maximum conservation?**
A: Because it likely fails politically. If industry rejects it, implementation collapses and we default to status quo (ecosystem collapse). Better to implement achievable 25% now and expand to 35-40% later if monitoring shows success.

**Q: What happens if Russia doesn't cooperate (transboundary issue)?**
A: We recommend parallel implementation. The Curonian is shared but each country can designate its own MPA. Precedent: Gotland Deep joint management (2001-present) works well. We recommend diplomatic coordination but don't require it for Lithuania to proceed.

**Q: How much will this cost my region/municipality?**
A: €3.1M over 10 years is national budget. Regional implementation costs are included. Individual municipalities' costs depend on enforcement role—estimated €20-50K/year for border monitoring and coordination.
